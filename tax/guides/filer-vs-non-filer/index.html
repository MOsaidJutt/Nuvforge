Filer vs Non-Filer: What Actually Changes

Filer status is not a badge. It changes the rate of tax deducted from you across a range of everyday transactions.

The basic difference

A filer is someone who appears on FBR's Active Taxpayers List, which happens after filing an income tax return for the relevant year. A non-filer is anyone who does not appear on that list, whether because they never registered, never filed, or did not file for that particular year.

The practical consequence is that non-filers face higher withholding tax rates on a range of transactions, and in some cases restrictions on carrying out certain transactions at all.

Where the difference shows up

Withholding rates commonly differ between filers and non-filers on:

  • Banking. Tax deducted on profit from savings and deposit accounts.
  • Property. Tax applied on purchase and sale of immovable property.
  • Vehicles. Registration and token tax on motor vehicles.
  • Dividends and capital gains. Deduction rates on investment returns.
  • Prize bonds and winnings.
  • Certain contracts and services. Deductions on payments received.

We deliberately do not publish specific rates here. Withholding rates and the restrictions attached to non-filer status change with each Finance Act, and an out-of-date number on a website is worse than no number. For your current position, check FBR's official rate schedules or ask us on WhatsApp and we will tell you what applies today.

The point most people miss

Filing a return is not the same as paying more tax. For a great many salaried people, tax has already been deducted at source by their employer throughout the year. Filing is the act of declaring it properly and appearing on the list. In some cases filing is how people recover tax that was over-deducted.

The common assumption that "if I file, I will owe money" is frequently wrong, and it is worth actually checking rather than assuming.

Is it worth becoming a filer?

If you own or plan to buy property or a vehicle, hold meaningful bank balances, invest, or run a business, the difference in withholding rates alone usually settles the question. If your financial footprint is very small, the calculation is less obvious, and we will say so rather than push you into a service you do not need.

Where to start

First, check whether you are already on the list. If you are not registered at all, start with NTN registration. If you are registered but have not filed, filing is the step you need.
