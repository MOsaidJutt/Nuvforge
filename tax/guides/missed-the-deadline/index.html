You Missed the Deadline. Now What?

Missing 30 September is common and it is not the end of the matter. Here is what it means and what you can still do.

The due date

The standard due date for individual and AOP income tax returns in Pakistan is 30 September following the end of the tax year. FBR has in some years extended this date. An extension is announced by FBR and should never be assumed in advance.

What missing it actually means

Two things follow from filing late, and they are worth separating:

  • You fall off the Active Taxpayers List. This is the consequence people feel immediately, because higher withholding rates start applying to banking, property and vehicle transactions.
  • Late filing consequences under the law. Penalties and surcharges can apply to returns filed after the due date.

We do not publish penalty figures here. Penalty amounts, minimums and the mechanism for restoration to the list are set by law and revised periodically. Getting this wrong from an outdated web page helps nobody. Confirm the current position with FBR or ask us for your specific case.

Can you still file?

Yes. A late return is still a return, and filing late is materially better than not filing at all. The longer an unfiled year sits, the more it tends to complicate the years that follow, particularly your wealth statement reconciliation.

Several years unfiled

This is more common than people assume, and it is workable. What matters is doing it in the right order, because each year's wealth statement builds on the previous one. Filing the most recent year in isolation while ignoring the gap behind it usually creates reconciliation problems rather than solving them.

If you have multiple unfiled years, tell us how many when you message. We will look at the whole position rather than quoting you for a single year that will not actually fix things.

The practical advice

  • Do not wait for next year's deadline. Waiting adds another unfiled year to the pile.
  • Gather documents for every unfiled year. The checklist is here.
  • Deal with the oldest year first where several are outstanding, so the reconciliation chain holds together.
  • Get the current year filed on time so the problem stops growing.

Next year, file early

The last two weeks of September are the worst time to file. Documents are harder to obtain, questions take longer to answer, and the portal is under load. Starting in July or August turns a stressful scramble into an ordinary task.
